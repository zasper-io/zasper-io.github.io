Compare

Zasper and Hex, side by side

Hex is a hosted analytics platform for data teams and the people they share their work with. Zasper is an open-source IDE for Jupyter that runs on your own machine. Both put an AI agent beside your notebooks; where they differ is where the work lives, what it is saved as, and who pays for the agent.

Question Zasper Hex
What it is An IDE for Jupyter notebooks A hosted analytics and BI platform
Where it runs Your laptop, or a server you run Hex's cloud, on AWS; a single-tenant deployment is an Enterprise add-on
Your notebooks .ipynb, read and written as Jupyter writes them, in your own folder Hex projects, saved as YAML; .ipynb can be imported, and exporting back to it breaks SQL cells and inputs
Languages Any Jupyter kernel: Python, R, Julia, C++, Scala, Go, Bash and more, with SQL cells beside them SQL, Python and R, with R not yet at parity with Python
The AI agent Claude Code, Gemini CLI, Codex or any ACP agent, on your own subscription; every change it makes can be reviewed and undone The Hex agent, with AI usage drawn from monthly per-seat credits; bring your own key on Enterprise
Agents outside the app An MCP server, on every install An MCP server, on Team and Enterprise
Git Built in: branch, stage, commit and diff, notebooks included Export projects to a Git repo, on Team and Enterprise
Price Free under the AGPL-3.0, with a commercial license for products that need one A free Community plan; Professional at $36 and Team at $75 per editor a month; Enterprise priced on request

Which one

Pick the one built for your work

They overlap less than a feature list suggests. The question is who the notebooks are for and where they have to live.

Choose Hex if
  • You publish dashboards and data apps to people who do not write code.
  • You want semantic models, scheduled runs and alerts managed for you.
  • Your team wants a hosted workspace with nothing to install.
Choose Zasper if
  • Your notebooks are .ipynb and should stay that way, in your git.
  • Your code and data have to stay on your machine or a server you control.
  • You already pay for Claude Code, Codex or Gemini and want it in your notebooks.
  • You work in kernels beyond Python, SQL and R.
  • You want it free and open source.
